# Pad Bowl, Key Trench and Borrow Pit on Depleted Excavators

**Site.** The LS-1 plateau, 60 m south of the main landing pad: loose regolith 50 cm deep over firm material. The crew is on its second sortie of the day, so the excavators arrive with partly depleted batteries and there is no recharging before the end of the sortie. The depot is on the south edge of the work area.

**Structures.**

1. Beacon pad: a circular bowl 12 m in diameter excavated 0.5 m to firm regolith (about 57 cubic metres), subgrade compacted. No sintering this sortie.
2. Dust berm: a 60 m straight berm on the north side, 1.2 m high and 3 m wide at the base, on a key trench 1.2 m wide and 0.8 m deep cut into firm material (about 58 cubic metres of trench), built from the pad spoil and the borrow pit, compacted in one lift.
3. Borrow pit: 8 m by 8 m, 0.9 m deep (about 58 cubic metres), next to the berm line, supplying the rest of the berm fill.

**Fleet.** EX1 and EX2 (excavators, 1750 Wh remaining each), HL1 (hauler, 2000 Wh remaining), CP1 (compactor, 2000 Wh remaining) and SV1 (surveyor, 1000 Wh remaining). These remaining charges are the whole budget for the sortie.

**Deadline.** 5 days.

**Operational constraints.**

- The three digs are of similar size; share them between the two excavators so that neither is loaded beyond its remaining charge.
- Only one robot may work inside a zone at a time.
- The surveyor stakes the pad and the berm line before excavation and inspects the pad after compaction.
- Berm compaction waits for the fill from the pad bowl and the borrow pit.
