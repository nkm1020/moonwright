# LS-1 Landing Pad and Blast Berm

**Site.** LS-1 is a graded plateau 1.8 km east of the Shackleton crater rim near the lunar south pole. The work area is roughly 80 m by 80 m of loose regolith to 40 cm over firmer material. The robot depot sits on the south edge of the site; there is no recharging infrastructure.

**Structures.**

1. Landing pad: circular, 20 m diameter. Excavate a bowl 0.4 m deep to firm regolith, level to plus or minus 5 cm, compact the subgrade, then sinter a 30 cm regolith surface.
2. Blast berm: a 40 m arc on the north side of the pad, 1.5 m high and 4 m wide at the base, built from pad spoil, compacted, with a sintered crest to resist plume erosion.

**Fleet.** EX1 and EX2 (excavators, 5000 Wh each), HL1 and HL2 (haulers, 3000 Wh each), CP1 (compactor, 3500 Wh), PR1 (printer, 4000 Wh) and SV1 (surveyor, 1500 Wh). Each battery is the robot's budget for the whole job.

**Deadline.** Both structures complete and inspected within 14 days of mobilisation.

**Operational constraints.**

- Only one robot may work inside a zone at a time (plume and dust safety).
- No sintering starts until the surveyor has staked the site and the compactor has finished the pad subgrade.
- The surveyor must inspect both structures before the pad is signed off.
