# LS-1 Landing Pad, Short Sortie

**Site.** LS-1 plateau near the lunar south pole, same footprint as the baseline landing-pad job. Loose regolith extends to 40 cm and must be removed across the whole pad footprint; the berm needs a key trench cut into firm material because the plateau slopes north. The depot is on the south edge of the site.

**Structures.**

1. Landing pad: 20 m diameter bowl excavated 0.6 m to firm regolith across the full footprint, with a compacted subgrade. Sintering is deferred to a later sortie when the printer arrives.
2. Blast berm: 60 m arc, 2.5 m high, 4 m at the base, on a 0.5 m deep key trench, built from pad spoil and compacted.
3. Approach apron: strip a 30 m by 8 m lane on the south side so the hauler can reach the pad.

**Fleet.** EX1 and EX2 (excavators, 5000 Wh each), HL1 (hauler, 3000 Wh), CP1 (compactor, 3500 Wh) and SV1 (surveyor, 1500 Wh). No printer. No recharging; each battery is the budget for the whole sortie.

**Deadline.** 7 days.

**Operational constraints.**

- Excavation dominates this sortie: close to 100 cubic metres of regolith must be moved, so both excavators share the dig and no robot may be loaded beyond its battery.
- Only one robot may work inside a zone at a time.
- Berm compaction waits for the pad bowl, which supplies the fill.
